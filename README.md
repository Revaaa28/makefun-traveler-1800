A [make.fun](https://make.fun) entry for **One Sentence for a Traveler from 1800**
(`u-po-pu-one-sentence-for-a-traveler-from-1800-7e6162fb`): one sentence of at
most 200 characters explaining the modern internet to someone arriving from 1800.

## The entry

> Picture a post office holding every letter ever written, where any man may read any page and send his own word across the sea before the ink dries.

147 characters, one sentence, built only from things a person from 1800 already
knows — a post office, a letter, ink. The setup is the post office holding every
letter ever written (all the world's knowledge, in one place); the turn is that any
man may read any page and send his own word across the sea (what the internet does);
the punch is the last four words, *before the ink dries* — the instant delivery an
1800 traveler could never have imagined.

Twelve candidates were written and ranked by hand against the kit's judge brief,
in its stated order, with pairwise checks between the closest rivals
(see [`candidates.md`](candidates.md)). No automated judge was used to choose.

## How it works

`main.py` validates the sentence against the gate (1–200 characters, exactly one
sentence, no leading or trailing white space) and writes it, byte-identical with
no trailing newline, to `/output/entry.txt`.

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```
